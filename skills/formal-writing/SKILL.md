# Formal writing

Goal: the reader understands the point in the first lines, knows exactly what to do, and feels respected.
Formal does not mean long or complicated. It means precise, complete and polite.

## 1. Before writing, answer four questions

| Question | Effect on the text |
|---|---|
| Who reads it, and how senior are they? | Sets the tone and how much background to include |
| What do they already know? | Do not explain what they know; do explain what they do not |
| What should they do after reading? | This becomes the opening and the closing |
| What is the one main point? | If it cannot be said in two sentences, the thinking is not finished |

When editing a user's draft, keep their meaning and facts. Change wording, order and tone only. If a fact
looks wrong or is missing (a date, a quantity, a name), point to it instead of inventing one.

## 2. Structure: point first

1. **Purpose or answer** - why you are writing, or the conclusion.
2. **Key facts** - only those that support the purpose, most important first.
3. **Action** - who does what by when.
4. **Closing** - one polite line.

Background comes after the point, not before. A reader who stops after the first paragraph should still
have the message.

## 3. Sentence rules

- One idea per sentence, about 15 to 20 words. One topic per paragraph, 3 to 5 sentences.
- Use the active voice and name the actor: `The quality team approved the sample`, not `The sample was
  approved`. Use the passive only when the actor is unknown or unimportant.
- Be specific: numbers with units, dates in full (`18 November 2026`), names of people and teams.
  `As soon as possible` becomes `by Thursday, 19 November`.
- Use the same word for the same thing throughout. A new word suggests a new thing.
- Write complete sentences with full forms: `do not`, `cannot`, `it is`.
- Explain an abbreviation the first time unless every reader uses it daily.

## 4. Tone

| Aim | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Polite | `Could you please send the report by Friday?` | `Send the report.` / `Kindly do the needful.` |
| Confident | `We recommend option B.` | `We kind of think maybe option B.` |
| Honest about doubt | `The cause is not yet confirmed; we will report on Monday.` | Hiding doubt, or doubting every line |
| Neutral on problems | `The delivery arrived two days late.` | `Your team failed again.` |
| Respectful refusal | `We are unable to approve this because ...; we can offer ...` | A bare `No`, or a vague maybe |

State bad news plainly, early, with the cause and the next step. Apologize once, specifically, then move
to the fix. Thank people for something specific, not out of habit.

## 5. Plain words

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| in order to | to |
| due to the fact that | because |
| at this point in time | now |
| in the event that | if |
| with regard to / in respect of | about |
| utilize / leverage | use |
| commence / terminate | start / end |
| prior to / subsequent to | before / after |
| please be informed that | (delete; state the information) |
| I am writing to inform you that | (delete; state the information) |

Also remove: slang, jokes, exclamation marks, emoji, intensifiers (`very`, `really`, `extremely`), and
filler openings and closings that carry no information.

## 6. Patterns

**Email**

```
Subject: <Topic> - <action needed> by <date>

Dear Mr./Ms. <Family name>,   (or the form of address the user's company uses)

<Purpose in one or two sentences.>

<Key facts: a short paragraph, or 3-5 bullets when there are separate items.>

<Action: who, what, by when.>

Best regards,
<Name>
<Title, team, phone>
```

- The subject line alone should tell the reader what the email is and what is needed.
- One email, one subject. Mention attachments by name.
- Replying to a senior person: answer their question in the first sentence.

**Short report or memo**: title, summary (3-5 lines), background, findings, conclusion, recommendation,
next steps with owner and date.

**Request**: what you need, why, by when, what you have already done, and thanks.

**Problem report**: what happened, impact, cause (or "under investigation"), action taken, action planned
with dates.

## 7. Check before sending

1. Is the main point in the first two sentences?
2. Does every action have an owner and a date?
3. Are all names, titles, numbers, units and dates correct and consistent?
4. Can any sentence be cut with no loss? Cut it.
5. Would the text sound right if read aloud to the recipient?
6. Spelling and grammar clean; one spelling standard (British or American) throughout.

Deliver the final text ready to paste. Add notes only for what the user must decide or fill in, marked
clearly, for example `[confirm date]`.
